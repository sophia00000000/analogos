    Methodology

    How do we measure similarity?

    Every site (on Mars or in Colombia) has a score from 0 to 1 on several criteria. Similarity measures how much of what makes the Mars site special is present in the Colombian place:

    similarity = Σ wk · min(Ek, Ck) ÷ Σ wk · Ek

    E = Mars site, C = place in Colombia, w = criterion weight for the profile.

    Two profiles

    Science (Jezero, Gale, Mawrth Vallis, Valles Marineris) gives more weight to water-related geology: clays, sulfates, salts, sediments and hydrothermal activity. Landing & base (Arcadia, Utopia, Deuteronilus, Hellas, Arsia) gives more weight to operating conditions: flat terrain, ice, aridity, thermal cycling, cold and surface stability. By default the site's own profile is used, but the selector in Step 2 lets you compare the same site under the other profile.

    Where the scores come from

    Geology (ice, clays, sulfates, salts, sediments, volcanic, subsurface, hydrothermal): assigned by the team from the literature; they are preliminary.

    Measured (aridity, cold, thermal cycling, surface stability, flat terrain): computed from NASA POWER and NASA MODIS data, normalized min-max across the 8 Colombian places. The place most similar to Mars on each variable scores 1 and the least similar scores 0:

    • aridity = 0.7 · inv(annual rainfall) + 0.3 · inv(relative humidity)
    • cold = inv(nighttime land surface temperature, MODIS)
    • thermal cycling = norm(day-night surface temperature range)
    • surface stability = inv(rainfall erosivity)
    • flat terrain = inv(mean slope) — slope not yet available

    norm(x) = (x − min) ÷ (max − min), inv(x) = 1 − norm(x). On the Mars sites these criteria equal 1, because Mars is the extreme. Since the scale is relative, a 1 means "the most Mars-like of the Colombian places", not "identical to Mars".

    Missing data

    If a place has no measured value for a criterion, that criterion is excluded from both the numerator and the denominator (it does not count as 0). Each result shows how many of the Mars site's criteria were used. If fewer than 60% were used, the result is flagged ⚠ incomplete data, dimmed and moved to the bottom of the ranking. Without this rule, Alta Guajira, which has no land surface temperature data, could score close to 100% on aridity alone.

    Sources of the measured data: NASA POWER Project; Wan, Z., Hook, S., Hulley, G. MOD11A2 v061, NASA LP DAAC; AppEEARS Team (2026), NASA LP DAAC.
